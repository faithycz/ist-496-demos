# US Public EV Charging Landscape Briefing

## How large is the public charging network?
The US public EV charging network has grown quickly and now includes **roughly 200,000 public charging ports** nationwide, according to the DOE’s Alternative Fuels Data Center and 2024 coverage summaries. This total includes both **AC Level 2** and **DC fast-charging** ports at public locations. The exact figure changes month to month as new sites open, but the key takeaway is that public charging is now a large and expanding national infrastructure, with strong growth in both urban and highway corridors.

## Two major charging networks a fleet can rely on
**Electrify America** is one of the largest open public fast-charging networks in the US. It is especially relevant for fleets because it focuses on high-power DC fast charging, which can reduce vehicle downtime. Public descriptions put it at **more than 5,100 charging ports at nearly 1,100 US locations**. Its broad highway and metro footprint makes it a practical choice for long-distance fleet travel and turn-around charging.

**EVgo** is another major public fast-charging network and is widely used in fleet and urban charging use cases. Public descriptions note **more than 1,100 charging stations across 47 states**, with a strong emphasis on fast charging in retail and city locations. EVgo’s network is useful for fleets that need reliable top-up charging in dense markets and along regional travel routes.

## Bottom line
For a sales fleet, the public charging landscape is now deep enough to support EV adoption, but reliability and site mix matter more than headline counts. A practical strategy is to lean on **Electrify America for high-power corridor charging** and **EVgo for urban and regional fast charging**, while verifying charger uptime, vehicle compatibility, and local site density for the fleet’s operating territories.
