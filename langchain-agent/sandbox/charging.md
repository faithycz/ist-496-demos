# US Public EV Charging Landscape for Fleet Planning

## How many public charging points are there?
The US public EV charging network has grown quickly. A reasonable current planning figure is **about 195,000 public charging ports across roughly 70,000 stations** in the United States. 

For a fleet operator, the key takeaway is that public charging is now broad enough to support mixed-route and regional operations, but availability still varies a lot by metro area, highway corridor, and connector type. Fast-charging coverage is improving, but fleets should still plan around depot charging for the bulk of daily energy needs.

## Two major networks a fleet could rely on

### ChargePoint
ChargePoint is one of the largest public charging networks in the US and is widely deployed at workplaces, retail locations, parking facilities, and some fleet-friendly sites. Its main strength is breadth: lots of AC Level 2 locations, plus a growing fast-charging footprint. For fleets, ChargePoint can be useful for destination charging and for drivers who need access to a large, familiar network.

### Electrify America
Electrify America is one of the most important US public DC fast-charging networks. It is especially relevant for fleet vehicles that need rapid top-ups on route, along highways, or between service calls. Its network is smaller than ChargePoint’s overall footprint, but it is a major player in fast charging, which is often the most operationally useful public charging category for fleets.

## Bottom line
The US public charging landscape is now large enough to support EV fleet adoption, especially when paired with depot charging and careful route planning. A practical fleet strategy would combine **private depot charging** with access to a broad public network like **ChargePoint** and a fast-charging network like **Electrify America**.
